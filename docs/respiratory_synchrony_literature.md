## 1. Respiratory Synchrony in Dyadic Interaction

### Paper 1: Yi et al. (2026) -- Causal Respiratory-Behavioral Synchrony

**Citation:** Yi, X., et al. (2026). Respiratory synchrony and individual differences causally influence dyadic interpersonal coordination. *Psychophysiology*, 63, e70254.

**Measurement:** Respiratory belt transducers during joint melody perception and production tasks.

**Synchrony Metric:** Phase synchronization of respiratory signals between dyad members; cross-correlation of respiratory and behavioral (tapping) time series.

**Key Findings:**
- Demonstrated *causal* directional influence from respiratory synchrony to behavioral synchrony using perturbation paradigms.
- Respiratory perturbations impaired both dyadic respiratory AND behavioral synchrony.
- Auditory-motor perturbations disrupted only behavioral synchrony (not respiratory).
- Partners with similar spontaneous production rates achieved better behavioral synchrony.
- Partners with similar resting heart rates showed stronger cardiac synchrony.

**Effect Sizes:** Not explicitly reported in abstract; significant perturbation effects.

**Predicted:** Behavioral coordination quality; demonstrates respiratory synchrony as upstream driver.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG (cardiac synchrony measured alongside respiratory).

---

### Paper 2: Muller & Lindenberger (2011) -- Choir Singing

**Citation:** Muller, V., & Lindenberger, U. (2011). Cardiac and respiratory patterns synchronize between persons during choir singing. *PLoS One*, 6(9), e24893.

**Measurement:** Elastic respiratory belt around chest (rib cage circumference); simultaneous ECG.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Phase Synchronization Index (PSI), Absolute Coupling Index (ACI), Positive/Negative Coupling Indices (PCI/NCI), Integrative Coupling Index (ICI), Granger Causality, graph-theoretic modularity.

**Key Findings:**
- Phase synchronization in both respiration and HRV increased significantly during singing vs. rest.
- Synchronization higher during unison singing than multi-part singing.
- Conductor showed causal influence over singers' respiratory patterns at high modulation frequencies.
- Different choir voices reflected in network analyses of cardiac and respiratory activity.

**Effect Sizes:** Very large -- PSI respiration eta-squared = 0.83 for condition; ACI respiration eta-squared = 0.67; ICI respiration eta-squared = 0.52.

**Predicted:** Interpersonal action coordination quality.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG, vocal audio, video, conductor hand acceleration.

**Sample:** 12 participants (11 singers + 1 conductor).

### Paper 3: Codrons et al. (2014) -- Spontaneous Group Synchronization

**Citation:** Codrons, E., Bernardi, N.F., Vandoni, M., & Bernardi, L. (2014). Spontaneous group synchronization of movements and respiratory rhythms. *PLoS One*, 9(9), e107538.

**Measurement:** Inductive plethysmography with two elastic belts (upper chest + abdomen).

**Synchrony Metrics:** Generalized Partial Directed Coherence (GPDC) across Low Frequency (0.035-0.15 Hz), High Frequency (0.15-0.4 Hz), and Very High Frequency (0.4-2 Hz) bands.

**Key Findings:**
- Breathing was more synchronized in collective groups than in individual controls, particularly at rest and during music-associated arm-swinging.
- Motor synchronization extended to spontaneous (un-cued) conditions on day 2.
- Thoracic respiration globally more synchronized in collective groups: F(1,2582)=4.41, p<0.04.
- **No cardiac synchrony co-occurred** -- respiratory synchrony was independent of heart rate coordination.

**Effect Sizes:** F(1,2690)=4.32, p<0.04 (abdominal); F(1,2582)=7.94, p<0.01 (thoracic, day 2).

**Predicted:** Motor coordination; spontaneous emergence of group behavioral coupling.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG (cardiac analyzed but not synchronous), arm movement accelerometry.

**Sample:** 60 university students (6 groups of 10).

---

### Paper 4: Boukarras et al. (2025) -- Dyadic Joint Action

**Citation:** Boukarras, S., Placidi, V., Rossano, F., Era, V., Aglioti, S.M., & Candidi, M. (2025). Interpersonal physiological synchrony during dyadic joint action is increased by task novelty and reduced by social anxiety. *Psychophysiology*, 62(3), e70031.

**Measurement:** ECG electrodes (cardiac only; no respiration measured).

**Synchrony Metrics:** Cross-correlation of ARIMA residuals from inter-beat interval (IBI) data, max lag +/-3 seconds.

**Key Findings:**
- Cardiac synchrony increased during novel task blocks vs. repetition.
- Social anxiety negatively predicted synchrony.
- No relationship between physiological and behavioral synchrony.

**Effect Sizes:** Conditional R-squared = 0.210.

**Predicted:** Task novelty engagement; social anxiety as moderator.

**Combined Modalities:** Movement kinematics recorded but not analyzed.

**Sample:** 40 dyads (80 participants).

---

### Paper 5: Konvalinka et al. (2011) -- Fire-Walking Ritual

**Citation:** Konvalinka, I., Xygalatas, D., Bulbulia, J., et al. (2011). Synchronized arousal between performers and related spectators in a fire-walking ritual. *PNAS*, 108(20), 8514-8519.

**Measurement:** Continuous heart rate monitoring (wearable HR monitors).

**Synchrony Metrics:** Recurrence quantification analysis of heart rate dynamics.

**Key Findings:**
- Fine-grained commonalities of arousal between fire-walkers and related spectators but NOT unrelated spectators during 30-minute ritual.
- Mediating mechanism appears informational (social bond), not behavioral mimicry.

**Effect Sizes:** Significant recurrence patterns in related pairs only.

**Predicted:** Social bond strength mediates physiological synchrony.

**Combined Modalities:** Heart rate only in this analysis.

**Sample:** 38 participants (12 fire-walkers, 9 related spectators, 17 unrelated spectators).

## 2. Respiratory Synchrony in Psychotherapy

### Paper 6: Tschacher & Meier (2020) -- Physiological Synchrony in Psychotherapy

**Citation:** Tschacher, W., & Meier, D. (2020). Physiological synchrony in psychotherapy sessions. *Psychotherapy Research*, 30(5), 558-573.

**Measurement:** Simultaneous ECG and respiratory belt monitoring of both therapist and client, with heart rate and HRV derived in 15-second intervals.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Windowed cross-correlation and correlation of local slopes (concordance), with surrogate controls using segment-wise shuffling.

**Key Findings:**
- Significant synchrony in respiration, heart rate, and HRV -- but NOT in raw ECG.
- In-phase HRV synchrony and respiration synchrony were associated with client's and therapist's alliance ratings.
- Respiration was one of three measures showing significant synchrony.

**Effect Sizes:** Not reported in abstract.

**Predicted:** Therapeutic alliance quality.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG + respiration (both measured).

**Sample:** 55 sessions, 4 clients, 1 therapist; average session 51 min.

---

### Paper 7: Gernert et al. (2023) -- Concordance Predicts Symptom Change

**Citation:** Gernert, C.C., Nelson, A., Falkai, P., & Falter-Wagner, C.M. (2023). Synchrony in psychotherapy: High physiological positive concordance predicts symptom reduction and negative concordance predicts symptom aggravation. *International Journal of Methods in Psychiatric Research*, 33(1), e1978.

**Measurement:** Skin conductance (4 Hz) via wristbands. No respiration measured.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Single Session Index (SSI) -- concordance rates (correlations of window-wise slopes).

**Key Findings:**
- High positive SCR concordance linked to symptom reduction (GSI decrease).
- Negative or small positive SSI linked to symptom aggravation.
- SCR synchrony explained R-squared = 0.429 (43%) of variance in symptom change.

**Effect Sizes:** R-squared = 0.429 for SCR synchrony predicting symptom change.

**Predicted:** Symptom severity change (GSI).

**Combined Modalities:** Skin conductance only; no respiration.

**Sample:** 14 dyads, 28 CBT sessions.

---

### Paper 8: Qaiser et al. (2023) -- Shared Hearts and Minds

**Citation:** Qaiser, J., Leonhardt, N.D., Le, B.M., Gordon, A.M., Impett, E.A., & Stellar, J.E. (2023). Shared hearts and minds: Physiological synchrony during empathy. *Affective Science*, 4(4), 711-721.

**Measurement:** ECG (two sensors below ribs), respiratory band (upper torso), skin conductance (palms), all at 1 kHz.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Non-directional concurrent synchrony via mixed-model analysis with linear/quadratic time trends removed.

**Key Findings:**
- SCL synchrony: significant concordant pattern (B=0.12, p<.001).
- IBI synchrony: present only when female partners disclosed (B=0.05, p=.03).
- **RSA synchrony: NO significant effects** (B=-0.03, p=.42).
- Physiological synchrony showed no robust associations with trait/state empathy.

**Effect Sizes:** Small to null for RSA synchrony.

**Predicted:** Empathy (trait and state measures) -- null result.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG + respiration + skin conductance.

**Sample:** 111 romantic couples (final n=95), 30-second epochs.

## 3. RSA and Polyvagal Theory

### Paper 9: Stuart et al. (2025) -- RSA Increases During Conversation

**Citation:** Stuart, N.C., Peters, B.J., Zoccola, P.M., Tudder, A., & Jamieson, J.P. (2025). Interpersonal conversations are characterized by increases in respiratory sinus arrhythmia. *Psychophysiology*, 62(3), e70043.

**Measurement:** ECG + impedance cardiograph (ICG) at 1000 Hz. Respiration derived from Zo impedance signal. RSA calculated via FFT with Hamming HF/RSA band 0.12-0.40 Hz.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Within-person RSA reactivity (not inter-partner synchrony).

**Key Findings:**
- Consistent RSA increases during both anticipation and actual conversations across 3 studies.
- Effects present regardless of: relationship type (strangers vs. partners), conversation valence (neutral/negative/positive), speaking role (speaker vs. listener).
- Supports polyvagal theory's "vagal brake" mechanism for social engagement.

**Effect Sizes:** r = 0.50 (anticipation), r = 0.34 (conversation) -- medium to large.

**Predicted:** Social engagement state.

**Combined Modalities:** ECG + ICG.

**Sample:** 356 dyads (712 adults) across 3 studies.

---

### Paper 10: Porges (2007, 2011) -- Polyvagal Theory

**Citation:** Porges, S.W. (2007). The polyvagal perspective. *Biological Psychology*, 74(2), 116-143.
Also: Porges, S.W. (2011). *The Polyvagal Theory*. Norton.

**Key Concepts:**
- RSA as non-invasive index of cardiac vagal tone via ventral vagal complex.
- The "vagal brake": RSA amplitude reflects vagal regulation supporting cognitive flexibility, emotion regulation, and social engagement.
- Social Engagement System: ventral vagal complex regulates both cardiac vagal pathway and striated muscles of face/head.
- Neurons in pre-Botzinger complex initiate respiratory rhythm and project to both nucleus ambiguus and phrenic motor nucleus, enabling precise temporal coordination of breathing, vagal gating, and cardiac deceleration.

**Measurement:** RSA derived from ECG (peak-to-trough or spectral methods).

**Relevance:** Provides theoretical framework for why respiratory-cardiac coupling changes during social interaction, and why RSA is a marker of readiness for social engagement.

---

### Paper 11: Interpersonal Cardiorespiratory Decoupling (2025)

**Citation:** (Preprint, 2025). The social, decoupled self: Interpersonal synchronization of breathing alters intrapersonal cardiorespiratory coupling. *bioRxiv*.

**Measurement:** Respiration and ECG continuously measured during bidirectional breathing synchronization, unidirectional synchronization, and pre-recorded signal conditions.

**Key Findings:**
- Interpersonal respiratory synchronization coincides with *cardiorespiratory decoupling* -- respiration and heart rhythms become more out-of-phase during interaction vs. rest.
- Stronger interpersonal respiratory synchronization predicts greater intra-personal cardiorespiratory decoupling.
- Trade-off: aligning with another person's physiology weakens one's own internal coupling.

**Implication for CADENCE:** When measuring interpersonal respiratory synchrony, intra-personal cardio-respiratory coupling should also be tracked as it may decrease -- this is informative, not artifactual.

## 4. ECG-Derived Respiration Methods

### Paper 12: Charlton et al. (2016) -- 314 Algorithm Assessment

**Citation:** Charlton, P.H., Bonnici, T., Tarassenko, L., Clifton, D.A., Beale, R., & Watkinson, P.J. (2016). An assessment of algorithms to estimate respiratory rate from the electrocardiogram and photoplethysmogram. *Physiological Measurement*, 37(4), 610-626.

**Methods Assessed:** 314 algorithms from combinations of 3 stages:
1. Respiratory signal extraction (filter-based or feature-based)
2. RR estimation (frequency-domain or time-domain)
3. Optional fusion techniques

**Best ECG Algorithm:** XB1,2,3 ET4 FM1 (feature-based extraction + time-domain breath detection + modulation fusion).
- Bias: 0.0 bpm
- 95% Limits of Agreement: -4.7 to 4.7 bpm
- Coverage probability (2 bpm): 80.5%
- **Four ECG algorithms outperformed clinical impedance pneumography.**

**Key Recommendation:** Time-domain RR estimation combined with modulation fusion. ECG outperformed PPG.

**Dataset:** 39 healthy participants, ~1,560 windows, reference = nasal-oral pressure.

---

### Paper 13: Varon et al. (2020) -- Single-Lead EDR Comparison

**Citation:** Varon, C., Morales, J., Lazaro, J., et al. (2020). A comparative study of ECG-derived respiration in ambulatory monitoring using the single-lead ECG. *Scientific Reports*, 10, 5704.

**Methods Compared (10 total):**
1. R-wave amplitude
2. R-to-S-wave difference
3. PCA
4. Kernel PCA
5. Q-R slope (upward)
6. R-S slope (downward)
7. R-wave angle
8. QRS slope range
9. Central moment (4th order)
10. QRS area

**Best Methods:** R-S slope, R-wave angle, and QRS slope range consistently produced lowest errors across all 3 datasets.

**Accuracy:** Correlation |rho| = 0.54-0.82 depending on method/dataset.

**Key Finding for Wearables:** QRS slope-based methods are computationally simple (require only R-peak detection, not full QRS delineation) -- ideal for ambulatory/wearable applications like Polar H10.

**Datasets:** 156 subjects, 59,482 one-minute segments (drivers, Fantasia, sleep apnea).

---

### Paper 14: Rogers et al. (2022) -- Polar H10 Respiratory Frequency

**Citation:** Rogers, B., Schaffarczyk, M., & Gronwald, T. (2022). Estimation of respiratory frequency in women and men by Kubios HRV software using the Polar H10 or Movesense Medical ECG sensor during an exercise ramp. *Sensors*, 22(19), 7156.

**Method:** Kubios HRV Premium v3.5 deriving respiratory frequency from ECG/RR intervals.

**Polar H10 Performance:**
- Correlation with gas exchange reference: r = 0.85 (SEE = 4.2 breaths/min)
- Median bias: approximately -3.9 breaths/min
- Limits of agreement: 8.2 to -16.0 breaths/min
- Clinically small adjusted median differences (~1 breath/min)

**Movesense Medical ECG:** r = 0.95, SEE = 2.6 (outperformed H10).

**Limitation:** Tested during exercise ramp to exhaustion -- breathing during seated dyadic interaction would likely yield better accuracy due to lower respiratory rates and less motion artifact.

**Sample:** 21 participants.

---

### Paper 15: Dong et al. (2021) -- EDR Evaluation Framework

**Citation:** Dong, K., Zhao, L., Cai, Z., Li, Y., Li, J., & Liu, C. (2021). An integrated framework for evaluation on typical ECG-derived respiration waveform extraction and respiration. *Computers in Biology and Medicine*, 135, 104593.

**Methods Evaluated:** 10 feature-based EDR methods including AMarea, AMQR, AMR, FMRR.

**Key Findings:**
- AMarea, AMQR, AMR: breathing rate errors below 2 bpm when sampling rate > 150 Hz; errors increase ~60% below 150 Hz.
- FMRR (frequency-modulation based): stable performance with error < 2 bpm regardless of sampling rate.
- Amplitude-based methods degraded ~40% in waveform correlation under noise.
- Window length: significant error increase when shortening from 32s to 16s for frequency-based methods.

**Relevance for Polar H10:** The H10 outputs RR intervals (not raw ECG waveform), so FMRR (frequency modulation of RR intervals) is the most directly applicable method. At the H10's native resolution, FMRR should achieve < 2 bpm error.

---

### NeuroKit2 Implementation (Open Source)

**Citation:** Makowski, D., et al. (2021). NeuroKit2: A Python toolbox for neurophysiological signal processing. *Behavior Research Methods*, 53, 1689-1696.

**EDR Function:** `ecg_rsp()` extracts ECG-Derived Respiration from heart rate signal.

**Available Methods:**
- `vangent2019` (default): 0.1-0.4 Hz bandpass filter on HR
- `soni2019`: 0-0.5 Hz filter
- `charlton2016`: 0.066-1 Hz filter
- `sarkar2015`: 0.1-0.7 Hz filter

**Input:** R-peak times or RR intervals from ECG.
**Output:** Continuous respiratory waveform estimate.

**GitHub:** https://github.com/neuropsychology/NeuroKit

---

### Polar H10 Breathing Analysis (Open Source)

**Project:** dont-hold-your-breath (GitHub: kieranabrennan/dont-hold-your-breath)

**Method:** Exploits respiratory sinus arrhythmia -- during inhalation heart rate slightly increases, during exhalation it decreases. Also uses 200 Hz accelerometer data from H10 chest strap to detect chest expansion/contraction.

**Key Insight for CADENCE:** The Polar H10 provides TWO respiratory signal sources:
1. **RSA from RR intervals** -- frequency modulation of heart rate by breathing
2. **Chest motion from accelerometer** -- direct mechanical measurement of chest wall movement

Both can be extracted without additional hardware.

## 5. Video-Based Respiratory Rate Estimation

### Approach A: Shoulder/Chest Pose Landmark Tracking

**Method:** MediaPipe Pose landmarks on chest/shoulders tracked over time. Vertical displacement of shoulder landmarks oscillates with breathing.

**Performance:** 98.5% accuracy on COHFACE dataset (MAE ~1.62 bpm, PCC=0.45 for motion-only).

**Requirements:** Face, chest, and shoulders visible and unobstructed for >= 15 consecutive seconds at >= 5 fps.

**Limitations:** Sensitive to body movement artifacts; works best for stationary subjects.

### Approach B: Dual-Modality (rPPG + Shoulder Motion)

**Citation:** (Video-based real-time monitoring, Expert Systems with Applications, 2023)

**Method:** Combines Eulerian video magnified remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) from facial videos with optical flow-based shoulder tracking.

**Performance:** 98.4% accuracy within +/-5 bpm tolerance for 15-second recordings.

### Approach C: Quality-Aware Multi-Method Framework

**Citation:** (Quality-Aware Framework for Video-Derived Respiratory Signals, arXiv 2512.14093, 2025)

**Method:** Concurrently runs 10 extraction methods spanning rPPG, upper-body motion, and deep learning, with quality-aware fusion.

**Key Innovation:** Uses MediaPipe facial landmarks for rPPG-based respiration AND Euclidean distances between upper-body keypoints for motion-based respiration, then fuses quality-weighted estimates.

### Massaroni et al. (2019) -- Contact-Based Methods Review

**Citation:** Massaroni, C., Nicolo, A., Lo Presti, D., Sacchetti, M., Silvestri, S., & Schena, E. (2019). Contact-based methods for measuring respiratory rate. *Sensors*, 19(4), 908.

**Methods Catalogued:** Respiratory airflow, sounds, air temperature, air humidity, air components, chest wall movements, and modulation of cardiac activity.

**Relevance:** Comprehensive taxonomy of all contact-based respiratory sensing methods with metrological characteristics.

## 6. Combined Respiratory + EEG Biofeedback

### Paper 16: Salminen, Jarvela et al. (2019) -- Social VR Biofeedback

**Citation:** Salminen, M., Jarvela, S., et al. (2019). Evoking physiological synchrony and empathy using social VR with biofeedback. *IEEE Transactions on Affective Computing*.

**Measurement:** EEG frontal asymmetry + respiration rate, collected via two amplifiers during VR compassion meditation.

**Biofeedback Conditions:** 4 solo + 4 dyadic VR scenarios crossing EEG adaptation x respiration adaptation (2x2 + avatar presence).

**Key Findings:**
- EEG frontal asymmetry biofeedback evoked higher empathy than respiration-only biofeedback.
- **Highest empathy when BOTH EEG + respiratory biofeedback simultaneously presented.**
- Stronger EEG frontal asymmetry synchronization correlated with greater reported empathy.

**Sample:** N=72 in laboratory experiment.

**Implication:** Respiratory and EEG signals provide complementary information for interpersonal synchrony; combined measurement is more informative than either alone.

---

### Paper 17: Chen, Hendrikse, Jarvela, Dikker et al. (2021) -- Hybrid Harmony

**Citation:** Chen, P., Hendrikse, S., Sargent, K., Romani, M., Oostrik, M., Wilderjans, T.F., Koole, S., Dumas, G., Medine, D., & Dikker, S. (2021). Hybrid Harmony: A multi-person neurofeedback application for interpersonal synchrony. *Frontiers in Neuroergonomics*, 2, 687108.

**Measurement:** EEG only (no respiration in current implementation).

**Neurofeedback:** Real-time inter-brain synchrony visualized as avatar head distance + sonified as chord pleasantness.

**Synchrony Metrics:** Six metrics: coherence, imaginary coherence, envelope correlation, power correlation, PLV, circular correlation coefficient, across delta/theta/alpha/beta bands.

**Key Findings:**
- Social closeness positively correlated with alpha synchrony (r=0.264, p<.001) and beta synchrony (r=0.210, p=.004).
- Platform is open-source and extensible to include respiratory and other physiological signals.

**Sample:** 236-243 dyads from Mutual Wave Machine installation.

**Implication:** Platform architecture could incorporate respiratory synchrony feedback alongside EEG. Authors note "movement and physiological synchrony may be both cause and effect of inter-brain coupling."

## 7. Implications for CADENCE

### Respiratory Signal Extraction for CADENCE

CADENCE already records Polar H10 ECG and video pose data. Respiratory signals can be derived from both without additional hardware:

#### From Polar H10 ECG (two methods):
1. **RSA-based EDR:** Apply NeuroKit2 `ecg_rsp()` with `vangent2019` method (0.1-0.4 Hz bandpass on RR intervals). Expected accuracy: ~2 bpm MAE at rest.
2. **QRS slope-based EDR:** If raw ECG waveform available, R-S slope or QRS slope range methods (Varon 2020) outperform across conditions. Computationally simple.
3. **Accelerometer-based:** Polar H10 provides 200 Hz accelerometer; chest expansion directly measurable as respiratory signal.

**Recommended:** FMRR (frequency modulation of RR intervals) achieves < 2 bpm error and is robust to sampling rate. For CADENCE's seated interaction context, RSA-based methods should perform well (low motion artifact, breathing rate ~12-20 bpm within 0.1-0.4 Hz RSA band).

#### From Video Pose (MediaPipe):
1. Track shoulder landmark y-coordinates (landmarks 11, 12) over time.
2. Bandpass filter 0.15-0.5 Hz (9-30 breaths/min).
3. Expected accuracy: ~1.6 bpm MAE for stationary subjects.
4. Can fuse with rPPG-based estimates from facial video for robustness.

**Limitation:** Video-based respiratory rate has lower accuracy and temporal resolution than ECG-derived. Best used as complementary/validation signal.

### Respiratory Synchrony as a New CADENCE Modality

Based on the literature, respiratory synchrony:
- Has large effect sizes in structured tasks (eta-squared 0.52-0.83 in singing)
- Has smaller but significant effects in naturalistic interaction
- Is a *causal upstream driver* of behavioral synchrony (Yi 2026)
- Is associated with therapeutic alliance in psychotherapy (Tschacher 2020)
- Operates independently of cardiac synchrony in some contexts (Codrons 2014)
- May produce intrapersonal cardiorespiratory decoupling during interpersonal synchrony (2025 preprint)

### Proposed Integration

**New modality for CADENCE pipeline:**
- **Respiration features (2-4 channels @ 1-2 Hz):**
  - Respiratory rate (from EDR or accelerometer)
  - RSA amplitude (vagal tone index)
  - Respiratory depth/amplitude (if accelerometer available)
  - Inspiratory/expiratory ratio

**Cross-modal pathways to test:**
- Respiration --> EEG (respiratory modulation of neural oscillations)
- Respiration --> Blendshapes (breathing affects facial movement)
- EEG --> Respiration (neural synchrony driving respiratory coupling)
- Respiration P1 <--> Respiration P2 (direct respiratory synchrony)
- Respiration <--> ECG HRV (intrapersonal cardiorespiratory coupling)

## Key References for Implementation

### ECG-Derived Respiration (highest priority):
- NeuroKit2: `pip install neurokit2` -- `ecg_rsp()` function
- Polar H10 accelerometer: `kieranabrennan/dont-hold-your-breath` GitHub
- Kubios HRV: commercial but validated with Polar H10

### Video-Based Respiration:
- MediaPipe Pose: landmarks 11 (left shoulder), 12 (right shoulder) y-coordinates
- Bandpass 0.15-0.5 Hz, count zero-crossings or peak detection

### Synchrony Metrics to Implement:
- Phase synchronization index (PSI) -- gold standard for respiratory synchrony
- Windowed cross-correlation -- simple, interpretable, used in psychotherapy research
- Concordance (correlation of local slopes) -- predicts therapy outcomes
- GPDC -- directional, frequency-resolved
